# Source Quality and Claim Grounding

## Evidence tiers

### Tier A — strongest
- original paper, book, patent, speech, archive, standards document, official record;
- official institution page directly responsible for the subject;
- museum/library/university archival material with provenance.

### Tier B — strong secondary
- peer-reviewed historical or scientific analysis;
- professional society or university explainer;
- respected scholarly reference work.

### Tier C — useful secondary
- reputable journalism with named sources;
- specialist publication with editorial accountability.

### Tier D — discovery only
- unsourced blogs, listicles, social posts, forum answers, autogenerated summaries.

Tier D may generate a search lead but should not carry a central factual claim.

## Claim rules

- Historical priority disputes: preserve uncertainty and attribution language.
- "First", "invented", "proved", "caused", and "never" are high-risk words; verify them explicitly.
- Quotes: fetch the source and verify exact wording. Prefer paraphrase when exact wording is unnecessary.
- Numbers: record unit, time period, and context.
- Current claims: prefer recent official or primary sources and record publication date.
- Anecdotes: verify the event, not merely that many websites repeat it.

## Independent-source test

Two sources are not independent when one clearly copies or cites the other as its only basis. Prefer different provenance chains.

## Evidence ledger statuses

- `verified`: direct support from credible source(s), wording matches evidence.
- `qualified`: broadly supported but requires uncertainty or narrower wording.
- `rejected`: contradicted, weakly sourced, or too ambiguous to narrate as fact.
