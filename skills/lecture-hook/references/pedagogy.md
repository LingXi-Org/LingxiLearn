# Pedagogy Notes

This skill uses a small number of robust principles rather than pretending storytelling alone guarantees learning.

## Curiosity via an information gap

A useful hook makes learners aware of a specific gap between what they currently understand and what they want to explain. The opening should therefore expose a *questionable state*, not merely announce that the topic is interesting.

Practical consequence: end the hook with a resolvable question or tension that the lesson can answer.

Reference starting points:
- George Loewenstein's information-gap account of curiosity.
- Pluck & Johnson, "Stimulating curiosity to enhance learning" (2011): https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/74470/1/Pluck_and_Johnson_2011_Curiosity.pdf

## Avoid seductive details

Interesting but instructionally irrelevant details can distract from learning. The skill therefore treats relevance as a hard quality gate rather than rewarding entertainment alone.

Practical consequence: every story beat must either create the conceptual question, sharpen the stakes, or bridge into the target concept.

Reference starting point:
- Sundararajan & Adesope, meta-analysis on the seductive details effect: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Keep-it-Coherent%3A-A-Meta-Analysis-of-the-Seductive-Sundararajan-Adesope/95806b02220471a55d9d18ebdc24d55d91973fd6

## Narrative is a vehicle, not the objective

Story structures are useful when they organize causal or conceptual relationships. Do not add characters, drama, or chronology unless they make the target idea easier to anticipate or remember.

## Level matching

- Novice: concrete scene, one tension, minimal names/dates.
- Intermediate: include tradeoff or misconception.
- Advanced: allow disputed interpretations, design constraints, or methodological limitations.
